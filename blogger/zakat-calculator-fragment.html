How Zakat works

Zakat is 2.5% of your zakatable wealth, due when your net wealth has been above the Nisab for one full lunar year. The Nisab is the minimum amount of wealth above which Zakat becomes due: 85 grams of gold, or 612.36 grams of silver. Only zakatable assets count — cash, gold, silver, goods for trade and money owed to you. Your house, car and everyday belongings are not counted, and debts you owe are taken off first. FAQs

When is Zakat due?
When your net zakatable wealth is above the Nisab and you have held it for one full lunar year (hawl). If it is below the Nisab, no Zakat is due.

Is Zakat due on my house or car?
No — things you use yourself, like your home, car, clothes and furniture, are not zakatable. Only wealth held as cash, gold, silver, trade goods or investments counts.

Which Nisab should I use — gold or silver?
Scholars differ. The silver Nisab is much lower, so it covers more people; many people use it to be safe. If you are unsure, ask a local scholar.

Is this religious advice?
No. This calculator only does the maths. For a ruling on your own situation, please speak to a qualified scholar.
